# Rumelt Strategic Diagnosis Framework

Based on Richard Rumelt's "Good Strategy Bad Strategy" — the most important strategy 
book for practitioners who need to cut through BS and find the real problem.

## Core Principle

Most strategy fails because it's not strategy at all. Rumelt identifies four hallmarks 
of bad strategy:
1. **Fluff** — Gibberish masquerading as strategic concepts ("synergize our core competencies")
2. **Failure to face the challenge** — Refusing to identify the actual problem
3. **Mistaking goals for strategy** — "Our strategy is to grow 30%" is not a strategy
4. **Bad strategic objectives** — A laundry list of things to do with no coherence

## The Kernel of Good Strategy

Every real strategy has three elements:

### 1. Diagnosis
The diagnosis replaces the overwhelming complexity of reality with a simpler story 
that identifies the critical aspects of the situation.

**How to diagnose well:**
- Look for the **crux** — the one challenge that, if resolved, would unlock everything else
- Ask "Why?" at least three times to get past symptoms to root causes
- Identify **structural** causes, not just situational ones
- Name the challenge explicitly — vagueness is the enemy

**Diagnostic Questions:**
- "What is the single biggest obstacle to growth right now?"
- "If you could only change ONE thing about your business, what would have the most impact?"
- "What have you tried that didn't work, and why didn't it work?"
- "What would a competitor say is your weakness?"
- "Where is there the biggest gap between where you are and where you should be?"

**Common Diagnoses for E-commerce Businesses:**
- "We're competing on price in a commoditized market with no structural advantage"
- "We've found a niche but hit the ceiling of market size"
- "We're spread too thin across too many SKUs/channels/markets"
- "Our growth depends entirely on advertising spend with no organic moat"
- "We have supply chain advantage but haven't translated it into brand or customer value"

### 2. Guiding Policy
The guiding policy is an overall approach to overcome the obstacles identified in 
the diagnosis. It channels action in certain directions without defining exactly what 
to do.

**Characteristics of good guiding policies:**
- It creates **advantage** by focusing energy and resources
- It works through **leverage** — applying strength against weakness
- It involves **proximate objectives** — goals that are actually achievable
- It uses **chain-link logic** — performance limited by weakest link
- It creates **coherent action** — all actions reinforce each other

**Examples of Guiding Policies:**
- "We will own the 'easy installation' position in solar mounting by making every 
  product installable without professional help"
- "We will build a direct relationship with solar installers instead of competing 
  on Amazon price alone"
- "We will use our factory advantage to offer customization that competitors buying 
  from trading companies cannot match"

### 3. Coherent Actions
Actions must be coordinated and mutually reinforcing. A list of independent 
initiatives is NOT a strategy.

**Test for coherence:** 
- Does each action support the guiding policy?
- Do the actions reinforce each other?
- Is there a clear sequence (what must happen first)?
- Are resources concentrated rather than scattered?

## The Crux Framework (From Rumelt's "The Crux")

Rumelt's newer work introduces the concept of the **Crux** — the most critical 
challenge that, once addressed, makes everything else easier or unnecessary.

**Finding the Crux:**
1. List all challenges facing the business
2. For each, ask: "If this were magically solved, would the other problems get easier?"
3. The challenge where the answer is most strongly "yes" is the crux
4. Verify: Is this challenge addressable? (If not, it's a constraint, not a crux)

**The Crux must be:**
- Specific (not vague)
- Addressable (you can actually do something about it)
- Critical (solving it unlocks other opportunities)
- Honest (not the easy problem you want to solve, but the hard one you need to solve)

## Application Template

When diagnosing a business, output in this format:

```
## RUMELT DIAGNOSIS

### The Situation
[Brief description of what the business does, where it is, what it's trying to achieve]

### Symptom vs. Root Cause
| Symptom (what they feel) | Root Cause (what's actually happening) |
|---|---|
| [e.g., "Growth has stalled"] | [e.g., "Market niche is saturated at current price point"] |
| [e.g., "Margins are shrinking"] | [e.g., "Competitors have caught up on product, forcing price competition"] |

### The Crux
[One sentence: The single most critical challenge]

### Why This Is The Crux
[2-3 sentences explaining why solving this unlocks everything else]

### Guiding Policy
[One paragraph: The overall approach to address the crux]

### Coherent Actions (in sequence)
1. [First action — must happen before others]
2. [Second action — builds on first]
3. [Third action — capitalizes on 1+2]

### What This Strategy Says NO To
[Explicitly list what the business should STOP doing or NOT pursue]
```
